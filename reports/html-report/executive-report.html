        Analysis

        What the Numbers Are Telling Us

        August 20265

        Membership is still strong...but.

        Membership YoY +25.0%

        Active membership is 525,382, up 25.0% versus July 2025 and up 81,139 year to date. The top-line membership position is still very strong, and the current projection has us on target to reach the year-end goal of roughly 554K active members.

        However, the growth is being driven more by strong retention than by stronger acquisition, with new golfer creation softer than last year.

        Recommendation

        The headline number is positive, but the next planning discussion should ask what is driving the increase, what is masking weakness, and where future growth will actually come from.

        Acquisition is softer than last year.

        New GC Golfers YTD -2.3%

        New GC golfers are down 2.3% year to date versus Jan-July 2025, and July new golfers were down 14.1% versus July 2025. That is the clearest soft spot in the current report.

        This does not mean acquisition is failing, and the cause is not yet clear. It could reflect a maturing growth market, a weaker-than-expected U.S. Open lift, or a problem with current marketing and conversion efforts. What is clear is that new golfer growth is not currently matching the strength of the overall membership number.

        Recommendation

        Make a deliberate effort now to identify the root causes behind slower acquisition and address them directly in 2027 planning. We should not make assumptions about the cause, but investigate it using the data available.

        Retention is doing a lot of the work.

        July On-Time Renewal 83.4%

        July on-time renewal is 83.4%, well above 66.0% last July, and July 12-month rolling retention is 79.0%, also above last year. The May auto-renewal changes appear to be the primary driver, helping protect the active base while new golfer creation is running behind last year.

        Recommendation

        This is a great development, but we should still look for incremental improvement in renewal communications where possible. We should also investigate the path to implementing auto-renewal across all AGAs for public members.

        Analysis

        What the Numbers Are Telling Us

        August 20266

        Recovery looks like a practical opportunity.

        Recovery YTD +30.8%

        Recoveries are 39,664 year to date, up 30.8% versus Jan-July 2025. Recovered members are coming from creation years as far back as 2022, which suggests expired members remain reachable longer than we might assume.

        If we invest in automating the win-back process and segment the lapsed member list and messaging, this could become a valuable way to help us grow GC membership long term.

        Recommendation

        Define what performance would justify automating win-back nationally, and investigate the time and cost required to build the plan.

        The 2027 Growth Question

        The last four to five years of GC membership development have clearly worked. GC membership has grown from zero to 525,382 active members, which is a tremendous achievement. GC has made getting a Handicap Index faster and easier, paid media campaigns have boosted visibility and demand, and the GHIN product has brought in tens of thousands of new members through product improvements and free trials.

        Affiliate marketing is a very smart 2026 and 2027 bet. It should have a measurable impact on GC membership growth because it will make the product more widely available, easier to buy, and easier to integrate into an existing golf app experience.

        But the current numbers raise a separate question: is access to the Handicap Index the main issue, or is new demand beginning to level off? If making the product more widely available is not enough on its own, should we consider additional ways to generate demand?

        Recommendation

        For 2027, answer the demand question directly: who buys a GC membership? What are the three primary reasons for that purchase? Can we use that answer to organize our resources and generate more demand? Generating new demand may require a new approach, more creativity, and some experimentation that makes us uncomfortable.
